MailerLite vs Kit: Which Email Platform Fits Your List?

The short answer

Pick MailerLite if you want a dedicated email platform at the lowest price per subscriber: the full feature set starts at $12/month, automation is included from the free plan up, and at 1,000 subscribers its Comfort plan costs $19/month — half of Kit's entry price. Pick Kit if you are a creator building an audience first and a business on top of it: its free plan holds up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends, and its paid plans are built around tagging, visual automations, and selling digital products to that audience. The trade-off is real in both directions — MailerLite's free plan is tiny (250 subscribers), and Kit's free plan holds a big list but keeps the automation builder behind the paywall.

Pricing as you grow

Both platforms bill on your active subscriber count, so both get more expensive as your list grows — but they start from very different places and climb at different rates. MailerLite's Comfort plan is the cheapest way to get full-featured email at every size we checked: $12/month at 500 subscribers, $19 at 1,000, $49 at 5,000. Kit's Creator plan starts at $39/month for up to 1,000 subscribers, and Kit's own billing guide shows how quickly the tiers step up: a list of 1,200 subscribers already pays the 3,000-subscriber tier price of $59/month. Kit's Pro plan starts at $79/month at the same entry tier.

Annual billing narrows the gap only slightly: MailerLite takes 10% off (Comfort at 500 subscribers drops to $10.80/month), while Kit gives two months free on annual plans (Creator's $39/month becomes $390/year). The structural difference matters more than the discounts. On MailerLite, moving between Comfort and Power buys headroom — send volume, automation count, support — not features. On Kit, the jump from free to Creator is what unlocks the automation builder at all, and Pro is a second jump for analytics and growth tooling. The free plans are opposites

This is the sharpest difference between the two platforms, and the one most comparisons gloss over. Kit's free plan is an audience-building tool: up to 10,000 subscribers, unlimited broadcasts, unlimited landing pages and forms, tagging, and the ability to sell digital products (at the standard 3.5% + 30¢ fee). What it does not include is the visual automation builder — automated sequences are where Kit starts charging. MailerLite's free plan is the mirror image: the automation builder is there from day one (3 active automations), along with a website and a landing page, but the ceiling is 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, and sending locks when you pass the subscriber cap. So the free-plan question is really a sequencing question. If you expect to grow an audience for a while before you need automation, Kit's free plan carries you much further. If you need a welcome sequence and a landing page working this week and your list is small, MailerLite gives you the machinery sooner — and starts charging sooner, too.

Automation and selling

Kit's reputation rests on this section, and it holds up on paper. Tags and segments are the organising system, visual automations and sequences are unlimited on both paid plans, and the selling layer — digital products, paid newsletters, recurring subscriptions, tip jars — runs through the same account at a flat 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction on every plan, free included. Pro then adds the analysis and growth layer: Subscriber Signals audience research, engagement scoring, advanced deliverability reporting, an insights dashboard, and a SparkLoop-powered referral system. It is a coherent stack for a creator whose email list is the business.

MailerLite covers more of that ground than its price suggests, with different gates. The visual automation builder is on every plan; the rationing is in how many workflows can be active (3 / 50 / unlimited) and in one specific line — multiple triggers per workflow are Power-only. Selling is present too: digital products and bookings on all plans, paid newsletters and recurring subscriptions on paid plans. What MailerLite does not have is Kit's creator-growth layer — no recommendation network, no referral system, no audience-research tooling. Its extras point the other way, toward small-business utility: a website builder with a blog, 100+ integrations, and surveys and quizzes. If email is one channel among several for a small business, that trade favours MailerLite; if the list itself is the asset you are growing, it favours Kit.

Who should pick which

Choose MailerLite if: price per subscriber is your deciding factor; you want automation, landing pages, and light selling working from a small list without paying creator-platform prices; or you are a small business that needs email plus a simple website in one subscription.

FAQ

Which is cheaper at 1,000 subscribers?
MailerLite, clearly. Comfort is $19/month at 1,000 subscribers and even Power — with unlimited sends and automations — is $39/month, the same as Kit's entry Creator plan. Kit Creator is $39/month and Pro is $79/month at up to 1,000 subscribers.

Which free plan is better?
They are built for different stages. Kit's free plan holds up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends but no visual automation builder. MailerLite's free plan includes the automation builder, a website, and a landing page, but caps you at 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month. Pick based on which limit you would hit first.

Can both sell digital products?
Yes. Kit sells digital products, paid newsletters, subscriptions, and tip jars on all plans with a 3.5% + 30¢ transaction fee. MailerLite sells digital products and bookings on all plans (1 on Free, 5 on Comfort, unlimited on Power) and adds paid newsletters and recurring subscriptions on paid plans.

What does Kit Pro add that MailerLite does not offer?
Audience and growth analytics: Subscriber Signals, engagement scoring, advanced deliverability reporting, an insights dashboard, and a SparkLoop newsletter referral system, plus collaborative editing and unlimited team members. MailerLite's Power plan competes on headroom instead — unlimited sends and automations — at a much lower price.

Bottom line

MailerLite and Kit are both subscriber-priced email platforms, but they are priced for different buyers. MailerLite is the budget tool that refuses to ration features: everything is in from $12/month, and the bill stays roughly half of Kit's at the sizes most small lists actually reach. Kit is the creator platform: a free plan that can hold a serious audience, automation and tagging designed around selling to that audience, and a Pro tier that adds growth analytics no budget tool matches. Start on MailerLite if the list is small, the budget is real, and you need the machinery now. Start on Kit if the audience comes first and the revenue is expected to follow it.
