Kit vs beehiiv: Which Newsletter Platform Should Creators Pick?

The short answer

Pick beehiiv if your newsletter itself is the product — you want a built-in website, recommendations, and an ad network, and you want to start free with room to grow. Pick Kit if email is one part of a bigger creator business — you sell digital products or courses, and you need serious automation and tagging to run it. Both are good; they are built for different jobs.

Pricing as you grow

Both platforms charge more as your list grows, but the shape is different. Kit prices by subscriber count, so a list that grows fast gets more expensive even if revenue does not. beehiiv's paid tiers are flatter and include the media features (website, ad network access) that newsletter businesses tend to want first. If you are under a few thousand subscribers, both are affordable; the gap matters at 10,000+ subscribers, where you should model your actual list size before choosing.

Automation and selling

This is Kit's home ground. Tags, segments, visual automation rules, and sequences make it the stronger choice if you run launches, sell courses, or segment buyers from free readers. Kit also sells digital products natively, so a small creator can run email and a simple storefront in one place.

beehiiv keeps automation simpler and spends its complexity on publishing and growth instead: a real website for your newsletter, a recommendation system that swaps subscribers with similar newsletters, and an ad network that can pay you once your list is big enough to attract sponsors.

Who should pick which

Choose Kit if: you sell (or plan to sell) digital products, courses, or coaching; you want detailed automations; email is the engine of an existing business.

Bottom line

There is no wrong choice here — there is a wrong fit. Sellers and automation-heavy creators belong on Kit. Newsletter-first publishers belong on beehiiv. If you are genuinely unsure, start on beehiiv's free plan, and move only if you hit an automation wall.
