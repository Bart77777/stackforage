The short answer

Pick systeme.io if you want one platform — and one flat bill — for your whole business: sales funnels, a website and blog, online courses, a community, and email, with a genuinely usable free plan and a $17 first paid tier. Pick Kit if email itself is the engine of your business and you want the deeper specialist tool: tagging, segmentation, a visual automation builder, and a creator recommendations network, priced by how many subscribers you have. systeme.io replaces a stack of tools; Kit does one job in more depth.

Pricing: flat tiers vs per-subscriber

The two platforms bill you for different things. systeme.io charges a flat amount per plan — $17, $47, or $97 a month — and what changes between tiers is your headroom: contacts (5,000 / 10,000 / unlimited), sales funnels (10 / 50 / unlimited), and courses (5 / 20 / unlimited). Its pricing page also advertises two months free on annual billing. Your bill stays predictable until you cross a limit, and then it steps up once, not gradually.

Kit charges for the size of your list. The free plan is unusually roomy — up to 10,000 subscribers — but the features that make Kit Kit, above all the visual automation builder, sit on the paid Creator and Pro plans, and their price scales with your subscriber count. Kit automatically moves you to the next tier when you pass your limit, so sending never stops, but a fast-growing list means a fast-growing bill even in months when revenue does not follow. At the entry tier (1,000 subscribers), Creator starts at $39/month on monthly billing or $33/month billed annually, and Pro at $79/month or $66/month at the standard annual rate (discounted in the first year when we checked). Check the official pricing page for the current price at your list size before you budget around it.

For a side project starting from zero, both can cost $0. The first real decision point comes when you need automations and selling switched on: that is systeme.io's flat $17 Startup tier against a Kit paid plan priced by wherever your list has grown to.

Features: all-in-one breadth vs email depth

systeme.io's pricing page lists the whole business in one feature table: sales funnels with upsells and order bumps, websites and blogs, online courses, communities, evergreen webinars (from the Webinar plan), CRM pipelines, physical products, and running your own affiliate program — plus the email layer of campaigns, newsletters, tags, automation rules, and workflows, with unlimited sends on every plan. The trade-off is depth per tool: the email automation, for example, is capped by tier (a single automation rule on the free plan, ten on Startup) and is built for straightforward funnel follow-ups rather than elaborate behavior-based branching.

Kit goes the other way. Everything on its pricing page serves the list: subscriber tagging and segmentation, a visual automation builder with split paths, automated sequences, conditional email content, engagement scoring and deliverability reporting on the higher tier, and a recommendations network that can feed you subscribers from other creators — or pay you for recommending them. Selling is built in too (digital products, paid newsletters, recurring subscriptions, tip jars), but there is no course platform, no community, and no funnel builder; Kit expects the rest of your business to live elsewhere and plugs into 100+ other apps. If email is where your money is made, that focus is the point. If email is one of five things you need this month, it is the gap.

FAQ

Which free plan is better?
They are free in different directions. systeme.io's free plan is a small business in a box — 2,000 contacts, 3 funnels, 1 course, 1 custom domain — with tight caps (1 automation rule, 500 students) and no expiry. Kit's free plan holds far more people (up to 10,000 subscribers) but is aimed at audience building; the visual automation builder is a paid-plan feature. Need to sell a first course for free: systeme.io. Need to grow a list for free: Kit.

Can systeme.io replace Kit for email marketing?
For basic-to-moderate email, on the published specs, yes: campaigns, newsletters, tags, automation rules, and workflows are included on every systeme.io plan with unlimited sends. Kit pulls ahead when automations get complex — its visual builder, segmentation, and engagement tooling are deeper, and automations are unlimited on its paid plans, while systeme.io caps automation rules by tier. The more your revenue depends on automation sophistication, the stronger Kit's case.

What does it cost to sell products on each?
systeme.io charges 0% platform transaction fees on all plans (standard Stripe or PayPal processing still applies). Kit charges 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction on all plans, including the free plan. On thin-margin digital products, that difference compounds; on high-margin offers, Kit's automation may earn it back.

How much does Kit cost as my list grows?
Kit prices its Creator and Pro plans by subscriber count and automatically upgrades you to the next tier when you pass your limit, so the honest answer depends on your list size at the time. At 1,000 subscribers, Creator starts at $39/month and Pro at $79/month on monthly billing. The official pricing page shows the current price for each tier — check it there before committing, and re-check it as you grow.

Can I switch platforms later?
Both advertise free migration: Kit offers a free migration service on its paid plans, and systeme.io lists free migration on its pricing page. Moving an email list is routine; moving funnels, courses, and a community out of an all-in-one is the bigger job, which is worth weighing before you build everything inside systeme.io.

Bottom line

This is breadth against depth. systeme.io is the better starting point for a new faceless or side-project business: one free-to-$17 path covers funnels, courses, community, and email, with 0% transaction fees and a bill you can predict. Kit is the better tool when email has become the business — its tagging, visual automations, and recommendations network are the specialist's edge, paid for with per-subscriber pricing and a 3.5% + 30¢ cut of sales. Start where your bottleneck is: no infrastructure yet, systeme.io; infrastructure fine but email underperforming, Kit.
