The short answer

Pricing: priced by list size, features are not the ladder

MailerLite's own summary of its model is that pricing is based on usage, not feature access: both paid plans include the full feature set, and the bill rises with your active subscriber count. What actually changes between Comfort and Power is headroom — how many emails you may send, how many automations, pages, and seats you get. One naming warning before the table: on June 16, 2026, MailerLite renamed Growing Business to Comfort and Advanced to Power, and repriced at the same time. Any review still quoting the old names is working from old numbers.

Two mechanics decide which plan you actually need. First, the send cap: Comfort allows monthly sends of ten times the top of your subscriber tier — 5,000 emails a month on the 500-subscriber tier, enough for roughly two sends a week to a full list. Power removes the cap (unlimited, subject to fair use). Second, the free plan shrank. In June 2026 it dropped from 500 to 250 subscribers and from 12,000 to 2,500 monthly emails, so Free is now a genuine starting tier — campaigns, 3 automations, a website, and a landing page — rather than a long-term home. Go past 250 active subscribers and sending locks until you upgrade or trim the list. For most small newsletters, Comfort is the realistic resting point; Power is the buy when you send often, run automations with multiple triggers, or want 24/7 live chat support.

Black Friday 2026: 26% off annual plans

MailerLite's Black Friday sale takes 26% off any annual plan for a full 12 months, at every list size. At the entry tier that puts Comfort at $107 for the year instead of $144, and Power at $222 instead of $300. The mechanics are unusually friendly: the discount applies to annual plans only, monthly-plan users can switch to annual during the sale and get the same 26%, and if your list grows and you upgrade mid-year, the discount carries over to the bigger plan. The sale runs from November 17, 2026 (14:00 UTC) to December 18, 2026 (16:00 UTC), and you enter a code at checkout. If you were going to pay for a year of email anyway, this is the cheapest week of the year to do it — the one caution is that it rewards committing annually, so it suits a list you already know you will keep sending to.

What it does

Email campaigns. Three editors — drag & drop, a simple editor, and custom HTML — plus a template library, a free stock image library, A/B testing on every plan, and email click maps. Paid plans add multivariate testing, an AI writing assistant, a subject line generator, and smart sending. Nothing here is rationed by plan in the way all-in-ones ration it; the editor is the same product at $12 as at $129.

Automation. A visual automation builder is included on all plans, free included, with e-commerce triggers and unlimited templates. The rationing is in active workflows: 3 on Free (up to 5 steps each), 50 on Comfort (up to 100 steps), unlimited on Power. Multiple triggers per workflow — starting the same automation from a form, a purchase, and a click — are Power-only, and that single line is what pushes automation-heavy senders up a tier.

Websites, landing pages, and forms. A website builder with a blog section, landing pages, embedded forms, and pop-ups are part of the subscription: 1 website and 1 landing page on Free, 10 of each on Comfort, unlimited on Power. Custom domains start on Comfort. For a creator whose "website" is really a subscribe page and a few landing pages, this can retire a separate page builder.

Selling. Digital products and bookings can be sold from the same account — 1 on Free, 5 on Comfort, unlimited on Power — and paid plans add paid newsletters and recurring subscriptions, alongside e-commerce integrations. It is not a store platform, but for a newsletter that sells one guide or one booking calendar, it removes a checkout tool from the stack.

The rest of the table. More than 100 integrations, dynamic segments and tags without caps, surveys and quizzes, and MCP access that lets AI assistants connect to the account. Support scales with the tier: knowledge base on Free, 24/7 email on Comfort, 24/7 live chat and priority email on Power.

Who should pick it — and who should skip it

Choose MailerLite if: you are starting a newsletter or small-business list and want real automation and landing pages from day one without paying specialist prices; your sending fits inside ten times your list size per month; you sell a small number of digital products or bookings alongside your emails; or you are comparing dedicated email tools on price per subscriber, where MailerLite's published ladder — $19/month at 1,000 subscribers on Comfort — sits well below the creator-platform end of the market. FAQ

What does the free plan actually include?
Up to 250 active subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with 2 user seats, campaigns in all three editors, 3 automations of up to 5 steps, 1 website, 1 landing page, 3 signup forms, and 1 digital product or booking. It is a real starting tier, but the caps are tight: pass 250 subscribers and sending locks until you upgrade or remove subscribers.

What is the difference between Comfort and Power?
Headroom, not features. Comfort ($12/month at 500 subscribers) caps monthly sends at ten times your subscriber tier, allows 50 automations, 10 websites and landing pages, and 3 seats, with 24/7 email support. Power ($25/month at 500) makes sends, automations, pages, and seats unlimited, adds multiple automation triggers, and upgrades support to 24/7 live chat.

Which plan should I choose?
Start free if your list is under 250. Choose Comfort if you send up to about twice a week and your automations start from a single trigger — it covers most small newsletters at $12–$49/month across the 500–5,000 subscriber range. Choose Power if you send more often than the 10× cap allows, need multiple triggers in one workflow, or want live chat support. Above 200,000 subscribers, pricing is custom on Enterprise.

How does the Black Friday discount work?
26% off any annual plan, at any list size, from November 17 to December 18, 2026, applied with a code at checkout. The discount lasts 12 months from payment and carries over if you upgrade to a larger plan during that year. Monthly plans are not discounted directly, but switching to annual during the sale gets the same rate — at the entry tier, Comfort drops to $107 for the year and Power to $222.

Can I cancel or move away later?
Plans are self-serve and priced month to month or annually, and subscriber lists are exportable as a standard email list. The rebuild cost of leaving is the usual one for a platform with a page builder: emails and lists move routinely, while landing pages and websites built inside MailerLite would need to be recreated elsewhere.

Bottom line

MailerLite's pitch is the mirror image of an all-in-one's: one job — email, with the landing pages and light selling that surround it — done with the full feature set at every paid tier and priced on a published per-subscriber ladder that undercuts the creator platforms. The honest qualifications are the shrunken free plan, which is now a runway rather than a home, and the two Power gates that matter in practice: unlimited sends and multiple automation triggers. For a newsletter or small business starting or growing a list on a budget, it is the easiest dedicated email tool to justify.
