This is a short list, not a directory. For a channel that never shows a face, two full video generators cover almost every format, and one voice platform covers the rest:

Best overall for voice-first channels: Fliki. The widest voice and language selection in the category, voice cloning on the entry paid tier, videos up to 15 minutes on Standard (40 on Premium), and a permanent free plan to validate your format before paying.

Best for turning existing content into videos: Pictory. Built to convert articles, blog posts, and scripts into finished videos, with premium Getty Images and Storyblocks footage, automatic subtitles you can export, and an unlimited number of videos on a minutes meter.

What a faceless channel actually needs

Strip away the marketing and a faceless video is four things: a script, a voice, pictures that move, and subtitles. The tools below divide that work differently, and the right pick depends on which part is your bottleneck.

Narration is the usual bottleneck. Viewers forgive stock footage; they click away from a robotic voice. That is why both generators compete on voices, and why a dedicated voice platform earns a place on this list. Footage is the second bottleneck — the difference between a generic stock library and premium libraries shows up on screen. Subtitles matter more than beginners expect: many viewers watch muted, and exportable subtitle files are part of a serious Shorts and repurposing workflow. Finally, the meter decides your real cost: a credits meter charges you for re-generating narration while you edit, while a minutes meter charges for finished video and lets you publish as many videos as the minutes allow.

1. Fliki — best for voice-first channels

Fliki is built around the voiceover. Standard ($28/month, or $21 billed yearly) includes 1,000 voices with 500 ultra-realistic ones, voice cloning, custom characters for a recurring on-screen identity, Series automation, and direct YouTube publishing. Premium ($88/month, or $66 billed yearly) raises the allowance to 600 credits a month and the voice count past 2,000 across 80+ languages and 100+ dialects. Paid plans include commercial rights and remove the watermark; Fliki states you can publish on YouTube and monetize, provided your text content is original.

The meter is the thing to understand before subscribing: credits are charged on the duration of audio generated, and re-generating after a script or voice change costs credits again. On Standard's 180 credits, Fliki estimates roughly 12 five-minute videos a month if you do not re-generate much — heavy editors will land lower. The free plan (3 credits a month, 720p, watermark, no commercial rights) is genuinely useful for one thing: proving your format works before you spend anything.

Pick Fliki if: your channel is narration-led; you publish in a non-English language or several languages; you want one cloned narrator voice locked in on the cheapest paid tier; or your videos run longer than 30 minutes.

2. Pictory — best for turning existing content into videos

Pictory's pipeline starts from material you already have: paste a script or a URL and it assembles scenes from its stock libraries, adds a voiceover, and burns in automatic subtitles, which you can also export as .srt or .vtt files — handy for Shorts and multi-platform repurposing. Starter ($29/month, or $25 billed annually) covers 30 minutes of video a month with an unlimited number of videos, 1080p exports without branding, and access to 5M+ royalty-free videos from Getty Images and Storyblocks. Professional ($59/month) triples the minutes to 90, expands the libraries to 18M+ assets, and adds voice clones and custom avatars. Pictory has already licensed the clips, music, and voiceovers it provides for use in your videos.

Two limits to plan around. Generated videos cap at 30 minutes on every paid plan, so long-form narration channels hit a ceiling here that Fliki's Premium does not impose. And while the ElevenLabs voices included on all plans are strong, voice cloning waits for Professional — if a cloned voice on a budget is the priority, Fliki's Standard or a standalone ElevenLabs account gets there cheaper. There is no free plan; the 14-day trial (3 video projects, 720p, with Pictory branding) is the evaluation route.

Pick Pictory if: your videos start as blog posts, articles, or scripts; you publish many shorter videos and prefer an unlimited video count; premium stock footage matters in your niche; or subtitle export is part of your workflow.

3. ElevenLabs — the voice layer to add when narration is the bottleneck

ElevenLabs makes no videos at all, and including it in a video-generator list needs that caveat up front. What it makes is the audio track: text to speech from a library of 10,000+ voices, voice cloning, and dubbing that re-voices content into other languages. Its pricing is the friendliest on this page — a free plan with 10,000 credits a month for testing (no commercial license), commercial rights from the $6/month Starter plan, and Professional Voice Cloning on the $22/month Creator plan, whose 121,000 monthly credits cover roughly two hours of finished narration. Language coverage depends on the model you pick, from 29 languages on the established Multilingual v2 to 70+ on Eleven v3.

The stack logic is simple. If your generator's built-in voices are fine, you do not need ElevenLabs. If your retention problem is "the voice sounds cheap," or you want one cloned voice that stays identical across tools and editors, a standalone ElevenLabs account is the cheapest fix on this page — and it is also why Pictory's built-in voices are as good as they are. How to choose your stack

Starting from zero, English, narration-led: validate the format on Fliki's free plan, then move to Standard when the watermark and missing commercial rights start to matter — which is immediately, if you intend to monetize.

You run a blog or newsletter: Pictory Starter is the direct line from articles you already wrote to videos, and its minutes meter suits a steady repurposing cadence better than a credits meter.

Non-English or multilingual: Fliki, on the published specs — 80+ languages with 1,000 voices already on Standard. Pictory's translation reaches 29 languages on Professional and 7 on Starter, and ElevenLabs' coverage depends on the model, so check your language against the model list before paying.

Voice is the problem, everything else works: keep your current editor or generator and add ElevenLabs Creator ($22/month) for the cloned voice and the narration allowance. It is the only item on this page that upgrades a workflow you already have instead of replacing it.

FAQ

Can I monetize faceless YouTube videos made with these tools?
On the tools' side, yes. Fliki's paid plans include commercial rights and explicitly allow YouTube monetization with original text content; Pictory licenses the clips, music, and voiceovers it provides for use in your videos; ElevenLabs' commercial license starts on its $6 Starter plan. YouTube's own channel monetization policies apply on top of any tool's license, so the content itself still has to be yours and transformative — a tool license is not a monetization guarantee.

Is there a genuinely free way to start?
Fliki's free plan is the only ongoing free tier among the generators: 3 credits a month, 720p, watermarked, and no commercial rights — a format test, not a publishing setup. Pictory offers a 14-day trial instead. ElevenLabs' free plan (10,000 credits) is useful for auditioning voices, but it is non-commercial and its dubs are watermarked.

Which tool is best for Shorts and vertical video?
Pictory explicitly exports 16:9, 9:16, and 1:1 on its plans, and its exportable subtitles fit a Shorts workflow. For longer faceless videos, the deciding factors are the ones above: Fliki's length limits and voice depth versus Pictory's 30-minute cap and repurposing pipeline.

Pictory already includes ElevenLabs voices. Do I still need an ElevenLabs account?
Not for the voices themselves — they are included on every Pictory plan. A standalone account is for control: voice cloning starts at $6 (Instant) or $22 (Professional) instead of Pictory's Professional tier, and the same cloned voice and library follow you across editors, shorts tools, and podcast or audiobook projects.

Which is cheaper for a small channel?
The entry paid tiers are close: Fliki Standard $28/month ($21 yearly), Pictory Starter $29/month ($25 annually). The real difference is the meter — Fliki charges credits when audio is generated, including re-generations, while Pictory charges finished minutes with unlimited videos. If voice alone is the gap, ElevenLabs Starter at $6/month is the cheapest paid item on this page.

Bottom line

A faceless channel does not need a shelf of tools; it needs one generator matched to its format and, eventually, one voice it owns. Start with Fliki if your videos are narration-led or non-English, Pictory if they are articles waiting to become videos, and add ElevenLabs when the voice — not the workflow — is what holds the channel back. Every pick above can be evaluated free or on a trial, so the cheapest research is to run your actual format through the free tier before subscribing to anything.
