Kinsta vs WP Engine: The Two Premium Managed WordPress Hosts, Compared

A note on links: we have no affiliate relationship with either host, so every link on this page is a plain link to the vendor. How our links work.

The short answer

Pick Kinsta if you want the more transparent ladder — published per-plan prices with visits, storage, and CDN bandwidth stated up front, free unlimited migrations, a built-in application performance monitoring tool, and a 30-day money-back guarantee behind a first month free on select plans. Pick WP Engine if you want the lower entry price on annual billing, the deeper WordPress ecosystem tooling (themes, plugins, and local development from its acquired brands), and a platform whose higher tiers add phone support and a 99.99% uptime SLA. Both are premium managed hosts solving the same problem — WordPress performance and maintenance handled for you — and both charge for it well above shared-hosting prices.

Pricing: two ladders, one shared meter

Both hosts price on the same two axes — how many WordPress installs you run, and how many monthly visits they get — so the honest comparison is per rung, not per brand. At the entry rung, WP Engine's Essential plans start lower: from $28/month billed annually for one site with 25,000 monthly visits and 10 GB storage, with the caveat printed on its own page that the headline figure is first-year pricing for new customers. Kinsta's Single 35k is $35/month at list for one site, but the rung is taller: 35,000 visits and 125 GB of CDN bandwidth included.

For multiple sites, Kinsta publishes the whole ladder — WP 2 at $70/month, WP 5 at $115, WP 10 at $225, WP 20 at $340, WP 40 at $450, with Agency plans from $340 and Enterprise from $500/month. WP Engine scales through its Essential tiers into Premium plans from $400/month billed annually, which add isolated resources, phone support, managed migrations, and the 99.99% uptime SLA. On both hosts, exceeding a plan's visit limit does not take your site offline — expect overage billing instead, which is the number to model if your traffic is spiky.

Performance and platform

Kinsta's differentiator is tooling visibility: a built-in APM (application performance monitoring) tool on its plans, continuous MySQL optimization, advanced caching, and a CDN allowance stated in the plan table, all behind a 99.9% uptime guarantee on standard plans. Its dashboard (MyKinsta) and API are built for owners who manage several sites and want to see what the platform is doing.

WP Engine's differentiator is the ecosystem around the hosting: AI-powered semantic site search, automatic page-speed optimizations for images and code, Advanced Custom Fields for building structured content, and push/pull tools for moving databases and media between sites. It manages WordPress, PHP, and MySQL updates for you, and its Premium tiers isolate your resources from other customers — the feature that matters when a neighbor's traffic spike is not supposed to become your slowdown.

Moving and safety nets

Migration is where the two hosts' confidence shows. Kinsta includes unlimited migrations with no downtime on its plans, plus free one-click staging environments. WP Engine includes daily automated backups and free SSL on every plan, and reserves managed migrations for its Premium tiers. Kinsta counters on the guarantee side: a 30-day money-back guarantee, and a first month free on select plans for new customers, which makes it the cheaper host to evaluate seriously. Both include staging; both take daily backups; the practical difference is whether migration help is bundled at your tier.

Who should pick which

Choose Kinsta if: you want to read your exact visits, storage, and CDN numbers off a published plan table before paying; you are moving an existing site (or several) and want migrations handled free; you want performance monitoring built in rather than bolted on; or you want a money-back window behind the trial.

Choose WP Engine if: the lower annual entry price decides it and your traffic fits the 25,000-visit rung; you build with the WordPress ecosystem tools it owns (custom fields, themes, local development); or you expect to grow into tiers where phone support and a 99.99% uptime SLA are worth paying for.

FAQ

What happens if my site exceeds its visit limit?
On both hosts the site stays online. Kinsta notifies you at 80% and 100% of your limits and applies overage fees based on actual usage; WP Engine likewise bills overages (its plans page notes taxes and overages may apply). If your traffic spikes unpredictably, model the overage rate, not just the plan price.

Can I host multiple websites on one plan?
Yes, on multi-site plans. Kinsta's WP plans bundle 2 to 40 installs at published prices, and its Agency plans start at 20 installs. WP Engine's higher Essential tiers add sites and visits, and its Premium and custom tiers are built for portfolios. A single-site plan on either host means one install.

Is there a free trial or money-back guarantee?
Kinsta offers a first month free on select plans for new customers, and all of its plans carry a 30-day money-back guarantee. WP Engine's headline entry price is first-year promotional pricing for new customers on annual billing — check the renewal price at checkout, because that is the number you will actually live with from year two.

Which host is cheaper?
At the entry rung, WP Engine starts lower ($28/month billed annually, first-year) against Kinsta's $35/month list — but Kinsta's rung includes more visits (35,000 vs 25,000) and a stated CDN allowance. At multi-site scale the answer depends on your exact site and visit counts; Kinsta's published ladder makes that arithmetic possible without a sales call.

Bottom line

These are the two default names in premium managed WordPress hosting, and the choice is transparency versus ecosystem. Kinsta publishes its whole ladder, bundles migrations and performance monitoring, and backs the trial with a money-back guarantee. WP Engine starts cheaper on annual billing and surrounds the hosting with the WordPress tooling it owns, reserving its best support and SLA for higher tiers. Either is a defensible buy for a site that earns money; the indefensible move is paying premium prices without checking which rung your visits actually land on.
