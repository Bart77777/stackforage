pCloud Review: Cloud Storage You Can Buy Once — If You'll Keep It for Years

The short answer

Pick pCloud if you are tired of renting storage forever and your archive is growing — raw video footage, photo libraries, project files. It is one of the few mainstream providers that still sells the same plans three ways: monthly, yearly, or as a one-time lifetime purchase, and the lifetime tiers break even against their own annual plans in roughly four and a half to five years. Skip it if you need deep office-suite collaboration (that is Google Drive and Microsoft 365 territory), if zero-knowledge encryption must be included rather than an add-on, or if a five-year payback horizon is longer than you trust any single provider with.

The break-even column is the whole decision. A 2 TB lifetime plan at $499 is simply five years of the $99.99 annual plan paid upfront: you come out ahead from year six onward, and behind if you leave, downsize, or the service changes before then. That is neither a scam nor a steal — it is a bet on your own habits, priced plainly. Two qualifiers matter. First, the lifetime prices above are the promotional prices displayed when we checked; pCloud discounts them further during its regular sales, which shortens the break-even — buying at full list price is the worst version of this deal. Second, "lifetime" has a contractual definition in pCloud's terms: the account holder's lifetime or 99 years, whichever is shorter. Practically that is not the constraint; the company's commercial lifetime is.

Family plans exist only as lifetime purchases, covering up to five users (2 TB was shown at $749 against a $999 list price when we checked). And if you want client-side encryption, budget for the pCloud Encryption add-on separately: $4.99/month, $49.99/year, or $150 as a one-time lifetime purchase, per the official pricing pages.

What it does

Storage, sync, and backup. pCloud covers the category basics: automatic backup and sync across devices, photo and file backup from phones and computers, and access from desktop, mobile, and web apps — including a Linux client, which several bigger rivals still skip. pCloud Drive mounts your cloud storage as a virtual drive, so files are usable on demand without filling your local disk first. pCloud states there is no file-size limit, which is the detail video creators actually hit on other platforms.

Sharing. Files and folders can be shared with anyone, and shared links can be protected with passwords and expiration dates — the controls you want when sending clients or collaborators large work files rather than posting them publicly.

Privacy posture. pCloud is a Swiss company, lets you choose whether your data lives in its EU or US data centers, and encrypts files in transit and at rest. What it does not include by default is zero-knowledge encryption: the pCloud Encryption add-on adds a client-side encrypted folder (the Crypto Folder) that pCloud itself cannot read. If your threat model requires that by default rather than by add-on, compare dedicated zero-knowledge providers before buying.

Who should pick it — and who should skip it

Choose pCloud if: you keep footage, photos, and project archives for many years and expect to keep growing them; you want the option to stop paying a storage subscription ever again; you work across Windows, Mac, and Linux; you send large files to clients and want password-protected, expiring links; or you prefer your data in an EU data center under a Swiss provider. Buy the lifetime tier during a promotion, and treat the free plan (up to 10 GB) as the trial.

Try pCloud

Skip pCloud if: your storage need is temporary or shrinking — the annual plan from any major provider is cheaper for a two-year horizon. Skip it if real-time document collaboration is the point (pCloud stores and shares files; it is not an office suite). And skip the lifetime tier if paying five years upfront for a single company's promise makes you uneasy — that instinct is exactly what the 14-day refund window and the annual plan are for.

FAQ

What does "lifetime" actually mean here?
Per pCloud's terms, a lifetime plan lasts for the account holder's lifetime or 99 years, whichever is shorter, and it is not transferable. The real-world limit is simpler: the plan lasts as long as pCloud operates the service. That is why the break-even math — about five years — is the number to judge, not the word "lifetime."

Is pCloud good for video creators' footage?
On the published specs, it fits the archive role well: no file-size limit, a virtual drive so footage does not have to live on your local disk, and 2 TB and 10 TB tiers priced below most rivals over a five-year horizon. It is storage, though — not a media-asset manager or a review-and-approve tool.

Is encryption included?
Standard encryption (in transit and at rest) is included. Zero-knowledge, client-side encryption is not: it is the pCloud Encryption add-on, priced at $4.99/month, $49.99/year, or $150 one-time for lifetime. If you handle sensitive client material, add it to the real cost before comparing.

Can I choose where my data is stored?
Yes. pCloud lets you pick its EU or US data region for your account. The company itself is based in Switzerland.

What if I change my mind?
pCloud offers a 14-day refund window on purchases. Note that discounted purchases may be treated differently under its refund policy, so read the terms on the checkout page before buying a promotional lifetime plan.

Bottom line

pCloud's subscription prices are unremarkable — $4.99 to $29.99 a month, in line with the category. What makes it worth a review is the checkout option nobody else in the mainstream still offers: stop renting. For a creator whose archive only grows, a lifetime 2 TB plan bought on promotion pays for itself around year five and then simply removes a bill. For everyone else — short horizons, collaboration-first workflows, encryption purists — the honest answer is an annual plan here or a rival better matched to the job. Decide by the break-even, not by the word "lifetime."
